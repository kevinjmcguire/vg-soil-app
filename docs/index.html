Water retention: van Genuchten (1980)

Suction h is positive (cm of water); matric potential ψ = −h. Effective saturation and water content are

Se = (θ − θr) / (θs − θr) = [1 + (αh)n]−m,   m = 1 − 1/n
θ(h) = θr + (θs − θr) [1 + (αh)n]−m

Inverting gives the suction at a known water content:

h(θ) = (1/α) · (Se−1/m − 1)1/n
ParameterMeaning
θrresidual water content: water held so tightly it does not drain
θssaturated water content: close to, but usually a little less than, total porosity
α (1/cm)related to the inverse of the air-entry suction; larger in coarse soils
npore-size distribution: larger n means a narrow range of pore sizes and a sharper drop in θ

Air-entry suction. The van Genuchten curve has no sharp air-entry point (unlike Brooks–Corey or Clapp–Hornberger). The value 1/α is used as an approximate air-entry suction: it marks where the curve begins to bend and drain noticeably.

Unsaturated hydraulic conductivity: Mualem (1976)

K(Se) = K0 SeL [1 − (1 − Se1/m)m]2
  • Classic Mualem–van Genuchten (default): K0 = Ks and L = 0.5.
  • Rosetta K0 and L: Schaap & Leij (2000) fitted K0 and L to measured unsaturated conductivity data. These usually describe unsaturated K better than extrapolating from Ks, but K at saturation then differs from Ks.

Pedotransfer functions: Rosetta3

The parameters θr, θs, α, n, and Ks come from Rosetta3 (Schaap et al., 2001; Zhang & Schaap, 2017). Rosetta is an ensemble of 1,000 bootstrap-trained neural networks.

  • Model 2 uses sand, silt, and clay.
  • Model 3 adds bulk density (0.5–2.0 g cm−3).

The value reported is the mean of the ensemble. The shaded band spans the 5th–95th percentiles of the 1,000 predictions; it shows how uncertain an estimate from texture alone really is. This page runs entirely in your browser; its JavaScript reproduces the USDA-ARS rosetta-soil package (v0.3.2) to better than one part in 107.

Water-holding points

  • Field capacity: θ at −33 kPa (337 cm suction) by default; −10 kPa is an option often used for sandy soils.
  • Permanent wilting point: θ at −1500 kPa (15,300 cm suction).
  • Plant-available water = field capacity − wilting point.

Units

1 kPa = 10.197 cm of water = 101.97 mm of water.   1 cm/day = 0.4167 mm/hr.

Using the app for the soil water homework

  1. Texture: set Sand (%) and Clay (%) from your soil description, with the slider or by typing. Silt and the USDA texture class are filled in automatically.
  2. Bulk density: leave Use bulk density checked and type the bulk density you calculated from the soil core (g cm−3). Rosetta then uses your soil’s actual packing, not just its texture.
  3. Water content: type the field volumetric water content, θ, you calculated from the core. Type it exactly; there is no slider to round to.
  4. Read the results in the boxes at the top, or in the Properties table tab:
    • Field capacity and wilting point: water contents at −33 kPa and −1500 kPa.
    • Hydraulic conductivity at your θ. Compare it with Ks: unsaturated K is often orders of magnitude smaller.
    • Suction (matric potential) at your θ, reported in mm, cm, and kPa.
    • Approximate air-entry suction, 1/α. On the retention graph it is the orange dotted line, where the curve starts to bend away from saturation.
  5. Units matter. Check what units the question asks for. The table gives suction in mm, cm, and kPa, and K in mm/hr, cm/day, and mm/s.

Things to try

  • Move the clay slider and watch how the retention curve and K change. Which soil holds more water at field capacity? Which drains faster?
  • Untick Use bulk density to see how much the estimate depends on packing.
  • Look at the shaded uncertainty band. How confident should you be in a hydraulic property estimated only from texture?
  • Switch the K model to Rosetta K0 and L and compare K at your θ.
  • Open Custom van Genuchten parameters and change n or α to see what each parameter controls.

About this app

This page runs entirely in your web browser: nothing is installed and no data are sent anywhere. It estimates van Genuchten–Mualem hydraulic properties with the Rosetta3 pedotransfer functions, ported to JavaScript from the public-domain USDA-ARS rosetta-soil package.

Source code, tests, and an equivalent R Shiny version: github.com/kevinjmcguire/vg-soil-app

Run the R version locally:

install.packages(c("shiny", "bslib"))
shiny::runGitHub("vg-soil-app", "kevinjmcguire")
